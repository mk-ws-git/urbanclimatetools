## Background

Phoenix regularly sees summer temperatures above 43°C (110°F), and heat is a leading weather-related cause of death in Arizona. After heat deaths reached nearly 500 in 2020, the city created a dedicated office in 2021.

## The heat response system

- **Office of Heat Response and Mitigation (2021):** a $2.8 million office, described as the first publicly funded office of its kind, led by researcher David Hondula
- **Heat Relief Network:** expanded to 112 cooling centres and 56 hydration stations
- **Cool pavement programme:** began in 2020, inspired by pavement sealant used in Los Angeles; later reporting counted about 100 miles treated

## What the evidence shows

- A later study questioned whether cooling centres reduce deaths, and city officials said they lack staff to evaluate their use regularly
- An Arizona State University study found the cool pavement raised the radiant heat a person feels by about 5.5°F on average, even though it lowers surface temperature
- The office's director said in 2023 that heat-related deaths were still rising, and officials reported more than 400 heat deaths in 2024 so far while crediting that summer's mitigation efforts

## Lessons

Naming and funding a lead office is a clear first step, but measuring health outcomes and testing each measure, such as cool pavement, is essential.
