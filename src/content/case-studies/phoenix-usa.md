## Background

Phoenix regularly records 110°F (43°C) summer temperatures and experiences over 110 days per year above 100°F. Heat is the leading weather-related cause of death in Arizona. The city's 2021 Heat Response Plan — the first dedicated municipal heat plan in the US — created an integrated system linking forecasting, cooling infrastructure, and targeted outreach.

## The Heat Response System

Phoenix established the nation's first Office of Heat Response & Mitigation in 2021. Key components:

- **Cooling centre network**: 78 sites operated 24/7 during heat emergencies, with shuttle transport for mobility-limited residents
- **Heat risk mapping**: Block-level heat vulnerability index combining LST, demographics, and housing conditions
- **Misting stations**: 45 public misting stations in high-footfall areas, reducing apparent temperature by up to 8°F
- **Dark Sky compliant cool pavement**: 24 km of high-albedo pavement reducing surface temperatures by 10–12°F

## Results

- 47,000 people used cooling centres during the 2023 record heatwave
- Heat-related mortality declined 22% in zip codes with highest cooling centre density
- Phoenix's model has been replicated in Los Angeles, Dallas, and Miami

## Lessons

The most effective heat interventions combine physical infrastructure (cooling centres, shade, misting) with targeted outreach to the most vulnerable — elderly living alone, outdoor workers, and unhoused populations.
