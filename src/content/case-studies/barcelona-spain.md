## Background

Barcelona's dense 19th-century Eixample grid left little room for greenery and exposed residents to some of Europe's worst traffic pollution and noise. The *superilla* (superblock) model, piloted in Poblenou in 2016, groups nine city blocks and restricts through-traffic inside them — freeing interior streets for people, planting, and play.

## The intervention

- **Superblocks** limit motor traffic to perimeter streets, reclaiming interior junctions as plazas
- **Green axes**: a plan to convert one-in-three Eixample streets into linear parks connecting green space
- New **trees, shade, and permeable surfaces** to lower street-level temperatures

## Results

In the Sant Antoni superblock, monitoring found **NO₂ concentrations down around 25%**, sharp drops in interior traffic, and measurable noise reductions within a year — with local retail activity holding up or improving.

## Lessons

Redistributing street space is a powerful, relatively low-cost climate-and-health intervention — but it requires careful attention to displacement of traffic and to "green gentrification" so benefits reach existing residents.
