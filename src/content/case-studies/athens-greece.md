## Background

Athens is one of Europe's hottest capitals, and heat is a "silent killer" that rarely makes headlines the way storms do. In 2021 the city appointed **Eleni Myrivili** as Europe's first Chief Heat Officer, tasked with coordinating a citywide response to extreme heat.

## The intervention

- A **heat categorisation system** ranks heatwaves by threat level — like hurricane categories — using heat, humidity, and health data to forecast danger by neighbourhood
- A pilot to **name heatwaves** to improve public communication and preparedness
- Restoration of the ancient **Hadrian Aqueduct** to provide non-potable water for irrigation and cooling, plus expanded tree planting and pocket parks

## Results

Athens created a role and a communication model now replicated worldwide — Myrivili was later named a global Chief Heat Officer. The categorisation-and-naming approach reframes heat as a nameable, rankable hazard people can prepare for.

## Lessons

Governance innovation — a dedicated officer and clear public risk communication — can be as important as physical infrastructure in reducing heat mortality.
