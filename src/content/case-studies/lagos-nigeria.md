## Background

Lagos sits on a low-lying coastal plain and barrier islands, and its Atlantic shoreline has been retreating for a century. Official Eko Atlantic project material attributes much of this to the blocking of coastal sediment transport by two breakwaters built at the entrance to the Port of Lagos between 1908 and 1912. Independent reporting describes further land loss since reclamation began in 2007, so the cause is disputed.

## The response

- **State plans:** the Lagos Climate Action Plan and Lagos Climate Adaptation and Resilience Plan (LCARP) target a 25% cut in greenhouse-gas emissions by 2035 against a 2020 baseline
- **Mangroves:** the state's environment commissioner has called mangrove afforestation a priority for slowing wave action and erosion, with restoration ongoing
- **Reclamation:** the Eko Atlantic development reclaims land from the sea behind a protective seawall, which supporters see as shoreline protection and critics link to wider environmental concerns

## Open questions

Published, independently verified figures on mangrove area restored, flood early-warning coverage or lives protected were not found. Anyone using this case should check current state reporting.

## Lessons

Large engineering projects and nature-based approaches can conflict, so cities benefit from independent environmental assessment and transparent monitoring of results.
