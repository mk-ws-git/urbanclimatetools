## Background

Melbourne's Urban Forest Strategy, launched in 2012, aims to cool the city and support biodiversity by increasing tree canopy cover in the City of Melbourne's public realm from a baseline of about 22% to 40% by 2040.

## The approach

- Mapping existing canopy to find the hottest, least-shaded areas
- A planting programme, with modelling suggesting at least 3,000 trees a year are needed to reach the target
- The "tree email" initiative, in which the city gave its trees email addresses so residents could report problems, which led to a surge of public messages to the trees and global attention

## Progress and obstacles

A 2018 council presentation put current cover at 27% against 41% potential cover. Later reporting found canopy cover in many precincts had not progressed far beyond 30%, and analysts have pointed to development pressure and limited space as obstacles. No official recent progress figure was found for this entry.

## Lessons

A clear numeric target and good mapping make progress measurable, but canopy growth takes decades and competes for space with development.
