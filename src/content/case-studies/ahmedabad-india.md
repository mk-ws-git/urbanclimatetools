## Background

A May 2010 heatwave pushed Ahmedabad above 46 °C and caused a sharp spike in mortality. In response, the Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation — with the Public Health Foundation of India, the Natural Resources Defense Council, and international researchers — developed South Asia's first city-level Heat Action Plan, launched in 2013.

## The intervention

- An **early-warning system** issues colour-coded heat alerts to hospitals, media, and the public days in advance
- **Public cooling**: temples, malls, and public buildings opened as cooling spaces; water distribution scaled up during alerts
- **Cool roofs**: high-albedo and lime-washed roofs piloted in low-income neighbourhoods to cut indoor temperatures
- **Health-worker training** so front-line staff recognise and treat heat illness

## Results

A peer-reviewed evaluation estimated roughly **1,190 fewer deaths per year** following implementation. The plan became the national template — India now has heat action plans in dozens of cities and states.

## Lessons

A low-cost, communication-first approach — warnings, coordination, and cool roofs — can save lives before expensive infrastructure is built. Sustained political ownership and annual updates keep the plan effective.
