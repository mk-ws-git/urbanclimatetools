## Background

Bangkok is low-lying, subsiding, and increasingly flood-prone as monsoon rains intensify. Landscape architect **Kotchakorn Voraakhom** and her firm Landprocess set out to prove that public parks can double as flood infrastructure.

## The intervention

- **Chulalongkorn Centenary Park** (2017), about **45,000 m²**, tilted at three degrees so gravity moves rainwater across the site
- A **sloped green roof, wetlands, and retention ponds** that collect, slow, and treat stormwater
- **Underground tanks** store harvested rainwater — enough to irrigate the park for up to a month

## Results

The park can hold up to **around one million gallons** of water during heavy rain, reducing runoff into surrounding streets. It has become a model for later Bangkok projects such as the Thammasat rooftop farm and Benjakitti Forest Park.

## Lessons

Multi-functional green space delivers flood mitigation *and* public amenity — a compelling value proposition for dense, land-scarce cities where single-purpose drainage is hard to retrofit.
