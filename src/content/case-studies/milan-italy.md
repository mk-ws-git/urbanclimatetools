## Background

The **ForestaMi** project was conceived as a metropolitan-scale response to summer heat and air-quality concerns. It grew out of research at the Politecnico di Milano, supported by Fondazione Falck and FS Sistemi Urbani, and is chaired on the scientific side by the architect Stefano Boeri. It treats trees as infrastructure for a whole metropolitan region rather than for the city alone.

## The intervention

ForestaMi's goal is to plant **3 million new trees by 2030** across the Metropolitan City of Milan, which is roughly one tree per resident. It is promoted by Regione Lombardia, the Metropolitan City and Comune di Milano, with the Politecnico di Milano, the University of Milan and Milan-Bicocca, Fondazione Cariplo, the Parco Nord and Parco Agricolo Sud parks, and ERSAF Lombardia. More than 130 municipalities, home to about 3.2 million people, signed up, and about 250 candidate planting areas were mapped.

Planting happens in parks, disused land, road edges and school grounds, and each season brings new projects. In the 2025–26 season, for example, ForestaMi announced ten new planting projects in nine municipalities of the metropolitan area.

## Results

- **About 611,000 trees and shrubs** planted in the metropolitan area since the 2018–19 season, according to the project's latest published figures as reported in March 2026 — "just over 20%" of the target.
- Reaching 3 million by 2030 means planting **more than 400,000 a year**, well above the pace so far; the same report notes that over two million are still to be planted.
- Project literature projects a **summer cooling of 2–3 °C** in parts of the city once the plan is complete, and the removal of "tons" of fine particulate matter. These are modelled estimates attributed to Politecnico di Milano research; we could not find the underlying study, and no measured cooling or air-quality results have been published.

## Lessons

ForestaMi shows how a city can work across administrative borders: over 130 municipalities share one planting target rather than each acting alone. It also shows the delivery gap that large tree-planting targets face — roughly a fifth of the goal after about seven seasons, with the remainder depending on land, funding, and survival of young trees. Treat the cooling and air-quality figures as design intent until measured results are published.
