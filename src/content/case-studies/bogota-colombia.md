## Background

At 2,600 metres above sea level, Bogotá's climate is temperate — but rapid urbanisation, traffic congestion, and stark inequality have amplified environmental stressors. The city's 2020–2024 Climate Action Plan explicitly linked climate resilience to its social equity agenda, a model that has since attracted study visits from 40+ cities globally.

## The Challenge

Bogotá's southern localities — home to the city's lowest-income residents — had 60% less green cover per capita than wealthier northern zones. Extreme rain events caused frequent flooding in informal settlements, while lack of tree canopy amplified heat stress in communities with less access to air conditioning.

## Approach

The city used the Urban Climate Risk Index to identify the 12 localities with the highest compound vulnerability scores. Resources — tree planting, green corridor construction, flood-resilient housing upgrades — were directed disproportionately to these areas.

The tree canopy programme targeted 120,000 new trees in five years, with species selection based on local climate suitability modelling. Community stewardship agreements employed local residents as tree wardens, combining ecology with income generation.

## Results

- 120,000 trees planted in target localities by 2023
- 18% reduction in heat-related emergency admissions in intervention zones (vs. 3% citywide)
- 45 km of enhanced green corridors connecting informal settlements to Bogotá's ciclovía network
- Featured in IPCC AR6 Chapter 8 as a leading equity-centred adaptation example

## Lessons

Co-designing climate interventions with affected communities produced higher adoption rates and lower vandalism rates than top-down greening programmes. The equity framing also unlocked access to multilateral climate finance that would have been unavailable to a purely technical infrastructure programme.
