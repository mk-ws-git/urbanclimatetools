## Background

Medellín sits in a valley, so heat and air pollution build up in its dense core. From 2016 the city began turning roads and waterways into green corridors, sometimes called "urban acupuncture": small, targeted greening of many sites rather than one big park.

## The Corredores Verdes programme

- More than 30 corridors covering about 65 hectares (a city news item counts 26 corridors tied to road axes)
- Initial planting of about 12,500 trees and palms, with many more plants, on streets and along waterways
- Native species and layered planting to create shade and habitat

## Results

Local government data report about 2°C lower temperatures three years after the programme began, and one official cited drops of more than 3°C in some zones. C40's Cities100 profile also projects a further 4 to 5°C decrease over 28 years as trees mature, but that is a projection. The cooling figure comes mainly from city and press reporting rather than a published peer-reviewed study, and a full air-quality evaluation was not yet available.

## Lessons

Many small, connected greening projects can be delivered quickly and cheaply. Independent evaluation of results would strengthen the case for copying the model elsewhere.
