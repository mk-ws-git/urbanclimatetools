## Background

Built on a drained lake bed, Mexico City faces a paradox: it floods and it runs dry. Over-extraction of groundwater and the compaction of the ancient lakebed cause **land subsidence of up to 50 cm per year**, cracking pipes and buildings, while depleted aquifers and leaky infrastructure threaten supply for millions.

## The intervention

- **Cosecha de Lluvia** ("rain harvest"), launched 2019, subsidises and installs **residential rainwater-harvesting systems**, prioritising water-stressed, lower-income boroughs
- Systems capture, filter, and store roof runoff for household use, easing pressure on the aquifer
- Partnerships with organisations such as **Isla Urbana** for installation and training

## Results

By October 2023 the program had installed **more than 62,700 systems**, making Mexico City the global leader in rainwater-harvesting installations. Studies estimate captured rainwater could meet a significant share of household demand in participating homes.

## Lessons

Decentralised rainwater harvesting is not a silver bullet for a sinking megacity, but at scale it reduces aquifer stress, buffers supply shocks, and builds household-level resilience — while the deeper subsidence and infrastructure challenges demand parallel, longer-term fixes.
