## Background

Accra floods almost every rainy season. The low-lying Odaw river basin drains much of the city into the sea at Korle Lagoon, but the channel is heavily silted and choked with solid waste, and informal settlement has encroached on floodplains. The 2015 June 3rd disaster — flooding compounded by a fuel-station fire — killed more than 150 people and made flood resilience a national priority.

## The intervention

The **Greater Accra Resilient Integrated Development (GARID)** programme, financed by the World Bank, began in 2020 as a roughly five-year effort now extended to the end of **2027**. It combines hard and soft measures: dredging and widening the **Odaw channel**, building primary and secondary drains across flood-prone corridors, a **flood early-warning system**, solid-waste management (compactor trucks and equipment to 17 assemblies in the basin, since uncollected waste is what blocks the drains), and upgrading of affected informal communities.

## Results (in progress)

- Drainage completed along several corridors (e.g. Accra Academy–South Kaneshie, linking earlier sections), with more under construction.
- The Odaw channel is reported about **40% dredged**; dredging resumed in late 2025 after a pause to clear spoil from the banks.
- Waste-management equipment delivered to **17 municipal/district assemblies** across the basin.
- A citywide flood early-warning system remains a 2027 target; independent evaluation of flood-damage reduction is not yet available.

## Lessons

Accra shows both the scale and the limits of hard drainage retrofits in a fast-growing coastal city: the engineering only works if paired with solid-waste management and floodplain governance, because debris and encroachment are what defeat the drains. It is an honest "in-progress" case — the infrastructure is being built, but verified flood-reduction outcomes should be reported only once the programme's own monitoring delivers them.
