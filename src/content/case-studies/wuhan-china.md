## Background

Wuhan sits at the confluence of the Yangtze and Han rivers and has long suffered severe waterlogging during the summer monsoon. In 2015 it was chosen as one of China's first 16 "sponge city" pilots — an initiative to let cities "act like sponges", absorbing, storing, and reusing rainwater instead of channelling it straight to overwhelmed drains.

## The intervention

- **389 sponge projects** in the first phase (2015–2017): permeable pavements, rain gardens, constructed wetlands, green roofs, and restored lakes
- Retrofits across **roads, residential communities, parks, and public buildings**
- Design targets to retain a large share of annual rainfall on-site and buffer medium-intensity storms

## Results

Modelling shows pilot districts trending toward the programme's **70% rainwater-retention** goal. Wuhan's experience is mixed and instructive: extreme events — such as the record rainfall of 2020 — still caused flooding, underlining that distributed green infrastructure complements, but does not replace, large-scale drainage.

## Lessons

Sponge-city measures deliver everyday stormwater and amenity benefits, but cities must size them honestly against extreme, climate-intensified storms and pair them with grey infrastructure.
