## Background

Rapid growth stripped Freetown of an estimated 70% of its tree cover, worsening heat, landslides, and flooding — including 2017 floods and landslides that killed around 1,000 people. In 2020, Mayor Yvonne Aki-Sawyerr launched **#FreetownTheTreetown** to plant a million trees.

## The intervention

- A **"pay-to-grow" model**: community "tree guardians" are paid as trees survive and grow, not just when planted
- A **digital tracking platform** geotags each tree and issues impact tokens that channel results-based finance to growers
- Funding assembled from the **World Bank, the Bloomberg Global Mayors Challenge**, and other partners

## Results

The city is on course to hit **one million trees by 2024** with a survival rate above **80%**, and the initiative was a **2023 Earthshot Prize finalist**. The tokenised-finance approach is designed to be replicable in other cities.

## Lessons

Tying payment to tree *survival* — verified digitally — aligns incentives and unlocks climate finance, turning reforestation into a measurable, self-sustaining programme.
