## Background

Paris is one of Europe's densest cities, with little vegetation and a severe urban heat island — the 2003 heatwave killed an estimated 15,000 people across France and remains the reference event for the city's heat planning. Schoolyards are among the largest publicly owned open spaces in the dense core: typically sealed asphalt, they heat up sharply in summer and sit empty outside school hours.

## The intervention

The **OASIS programme** (Openness, Adaptation, Sensitisation, Innovation and Social ties) re-designs schoolyards as climate shelters — removing impermeable asphalt, planting trees and shade, adding water features and permeable ground, and involving pupils in the design. Ten pilot yards (four kindergartens, four primary and two middle schools) were delivered under an EU Urban Innovative Actions grant of about €5 million between 2018 and 2021. The redesigned yards are intended to open to the surrounding neighbourhood as **cool islands** during heatwaves.

## Results

- **10 pilot schoolyards** transformed under the UIA project (2018–2021).
- **~75 schoolyards** greened across Paris by 2022 as the programme scaled beyond the pilots.
- The city's stated goal is to convert its roughly **700 schoolyards** into cool islands, with all public schoolyards targeted by 2050.
- A GIS screening of ~670 facilities identified dozens with high cooling potential, helping the city prioritise which yards to convert first.

## Lessons

Schoolyards are a replicable, publicly controlled asset almost every city already owns — converting them needs no land acquisition and pairs climate adaptation with everyday benefits for children. Paris's experience also shows the limits: per-yard costs are significant, and opening yards to the public out of hours requires governance and staffing arrangements as much as design.
