## Background

Hurricane Sandy struck in October 2012. The city's 2013 report found that about 51 square miles, 17% of New York City's land, flooded. The storm caused 43 deaths in the city, left nearly 2 million people without power, and the city later put damage and lost economic activity at about $19 billion. An NRDC analysis found that nearly 65% of the area inundated lay outside FEMA's 100-year flood zone, which exposed gaps in how flood risk was mapped.

## The response

- **Resilience plan (2013):** the Special Initiative for Rebuilding and Resiliency published "A Stronger, More Resilient New York", a 440-page report identifying 15 critical areas
- **Rebuild by Design:** a federal competition that awarded the city $335 million in 2014 for the first phase of "The Big U", a protective system for Lower Manhattan
- **East Side Coastal Resiliency (ESCR):** flood walls and park redevelopment from East 25th Street to Montgomery Street, which broke ground in autumn 2020 and covers more than 110,000 New Yorkers including 28,000 public housing residents

## Challenges

Costs and design changed along the way. Early reporting put the project near $760 million; a 2018 redesign that raised the entire East River Park added about $690 million, and a later figure of $1.45 billion has been cited. The redesign also drew community concern about closing and rebuilding the park. Check current city figures for the latest costs and schedule.

## Lessons

Flood maps based on past events can miss much of the exposed area, so cities need to plan beyond official flood zones. Large coastal projects also need sustained funding and community buy-in to stay on schedule.
