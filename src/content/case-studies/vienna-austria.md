## Background

Vienna, consistently ranked among the world's most liveable cities, is warming quickly and faces intensifying summer heat. Rather than a single flagship project, the city pursued a **layered network of small interventions** across public space.

## The intervention

- **Cool Streets**: car-free stretches with shade, seating, and cooling elements; sensors release spray-cooling when temperatures exceed a set threshold
- **22 "cool zones"** open in summer, roughly **100 mist installations**, and ~1,600 drinking fountains
- Planting up to **~4,500 trees per year**, green facades, and a goal of green space every 250 metres

## Results

Research on Vienna's nature-based measures found street trees lower daytime air temperatures by **up to 1.2 °C**, with **over 2 °C** of relief in shaded spots — with tree cover the single most effective measure. Roughly **85,500 m²** of streets and squares have been greened.

## Lessons

A distributed, repeatable toolkit — cool streets, mist, fountains, and above all trees — scales across a whole city and reaches people where they walk, complementing large green corridors.
