## Overview

The National Risk Index is FEMA's free dataset and online map showing which US communities are most at risk from 18 natural hazards. Rather than a single-hazard view, it combines expected losses with who is exposed and how well a place can cope — scored as **Expected Annual Loss × Social Vulnerability ÷ Community Resilience** — so two places with the same physical hazard can carry very different risk.

## What it does

- **Multi-hazard:** covers riverine and coastal flooding, heat waves, cold waves, hurricanes, wildfire, drought and more — useful when a city's exposure spans several hazards at once.
- **Composite risk:** blends hazard, social vulnerability and resilience into a comparable score at county and census-tract scale.
- **Downloadable:** export the data or pull county/tract reports for plans and grant applications.

## Who it's for

US local and regional planners, hazard-mitigation and resilience teams, and grant writers who need a defensible, standardised risk baseline. Coverage is US-only; for international multi-hazard screening see ThinkHazard! in this catalogue.

## Getting started

Open the map at hazards.fema.gov/nri, search your county or tract, and read the per-hazard scores — or download the dataset for analysis. Free and public.
