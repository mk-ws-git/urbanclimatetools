## Overview

Coastal Resilience is The Nature Conservancy's web-mapping decision-support platform (now part of its Natural Solutions Toolkit) for assessing coastal hazards and finding **nature-based** ways to reduce them. It is built around a four-step method: assess hazard risk and community vulnerability, identify nature-based solutions, take restoration action, and measure how much those actions cut flood risk.

## What it does

- **Risk & vulnerability mapping:** run coastal flood and sea-level scenarios at different scales and see impacts to critical facilities, vulnerable populations and ecological features.
- **Nature-based solution screening:** regional apps such as the Living Shoreline Explorer and marsh vulnerability indices help decide where reefs, wetlands or living shorelines are viable alternatives (or complements) to sea walls.
- **Measure effectiveness:** compare interventions by their modelled reduction in flood risk.

Projects span 17 US coastal states plus the Caribbean, Mexico, Central America and Indonesia.

## Who it's for

Coastal planners, conservation practitioners and resilience teams weighing grey vs. green shoreline protection. Pairs naturally with NOAA Digital Coast (data) and the case studies on green and coastal adaptation in this catalogue.

## Getting started

Start at the program site, then open the regional mapping portal (maps.coastalresilience.org) for your coast. Free to use.
